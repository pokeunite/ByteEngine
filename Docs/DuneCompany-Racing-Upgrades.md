## Reusable engine pieces

* **Route Path**: ordered world-space points, corridor width, gate radius. Inspector point editing and child-object import avoid hand-editing JSON.
* **AI Vehicle Agent**: ordered route progress, speed-dependent lookahead, speed reduction for corners, local avoidance, reverse recovery, start/stop and progress/state events.
* **IAiVehicleDriver**: an adapter supplies actual position/velocity and accepts throttle, steering and brake. The brain never writes a dynamic vehicle's transform.
* **Race Director**: countdown, swept gate checks in order, race position and finish event. Rules do not depend on Dune parts or currency.
* **Cable Renderer**: bounded round tube, slack sag and a metal hook. Dune's winch launches the hook before applying its physical tension force.
* **Rope Winch**: native Physics component for a reelable cable between rigid bodies or a world anchor. Includes local mounting points, tension-only spring/damping, capped pull, optional break force, hook flight and a round cable visual. Forces apply at mounting points and create torque. Dune's Bepu vehicle adapter shares its native tension law while retaining its independent assembly controls.

The Dune adapter registers independently powered assemblies in one `LandVehiclePhysics` simulation. Inputs, motors and reset boundaries belong to each assembly; contacts still use the same solver. Recovery targets remain passive and their resets cannot reset rivals.

This is original ByteEngine code inspired by Unreal's separation of AI brain, path following and movement ownership. Reviewed UE5 sources are under `D:/ue4 sources/UnrealEngine-5.8`; Unreal implementation code was not imported into ByteEngine.

## Garage and spring-arm polish

The inspector background joins the top edge; its content retains its previous spacing. The garage wallet sits 16 logical pixels from the right edge. Long-line winches have a wide spool and tall orange cage; power winches have reinforced cheeks, a reduction cover and twin motor housing. Stock engine art is repaired by `Tools/DuneCompany/repair_stock_engines.py`: the heavy engine uses a continuous belt with curved pulley wraps, and both stock engines have visible pulley timing marks. Engines animate at idle in the garage and increase their visual pulley speed while driving.

The 0.20.1 fix softened the lateral and angular guides too far. A saved two-engine vehicle with rear spring arms and truck tyres reproduced guide displacement at rest. Spring-arm guides now retain their structural stiffness at 120 Hz, with 30 m/s lateral and 40 rad/s angular recovery. A simulation containing spring arms uses at least 12 solver iterations and four substeps; other builds retain their existing profile. This costs additional solver work only while these mechanisms are present. Independently controlled assemblies also upgrade the profile when they introduce spring arms.

Regression checks measure the entire spring-arm mount rotation, as well as the tyre axle and lateral displacement. The saved vehicle passes 10 seconds of settling and 15 seconds of full-throttle alternating steering, including landing on a fallback floor: idle speed under 0.009 m/s, peak guide displacement 0.0258 m and mount/axle angle under 3.9 degrees. A four-arm vehicle also passes (0.0203 m and 2.61 degrees during the driving stress test). The tests use private fixtures and never write player saves.

## Tuning verification

The rebuilt 0.20.2 runtime passed Chrome testing with the saved two-engine, rear-arm vehicle: mount rotation error was 0.143 degrees at rest and 0.317 degrees during driving and turning; lateral error was 0.00018 m and 0.0050 m respectively. The private fixture cooked the upgraded engine model before testing. Browser diagnostics report the effective 12-iteration/four-substep profile and mount rotation error. This stronger profile has a measurable CPU cost: this headless run measured about 29 ms of physics on a frame containing two fixed simulation steps. No shipping game export was created.

All five upgrade types expose visible, disabled settings before payment. Unlocking charges the catalog cost once per type and immediately enables editing. Shop controls are hidden while tuning, leaving winch key controls unobstructed. The project now uses only Plugins/bytebard.dunecompany.byteplugin; the duplicate older package is preserved outside the plugin search path in .byteengine/backups.


## Web polish in 0.20.3

The browser now carries SandImprintTexture and its world origin through material caching and applies rut edge normals and trough darkening. Dynamic mesh updates skip unchanged index arrays and reuse vertex buffers; hidden mesh residency is bounded to 32 MiB and 120 frames. Spring arms retain four substeps and firm guides, using eight iterations for normal motion and twelve when vertical motion, chassis rotation or mount error indicates stress. Explicit higher iteration requests are preserved. A terrain simplification experiment was rejected because it reduced deep-pit extraction; collision geometry retains its original 1 mm fidelity.

Chrome passed the loaded vehicle and track texture checks with no WebGL errors. Native recovery checks produced 0.089 m grooves and pulled the deep-pit casualty 4.09 m. Loaded suspension stress stayed under 5.01 cm lateral error and 3.82 degrees. The local mesh upload comparison fell from approximately 104 KB to 61 KB per frame; timing runs were affected by concurrent Chrome CPU/GPU load and do not establish an itch FPS improvement.
