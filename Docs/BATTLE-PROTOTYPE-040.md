# Goblin Scraper: battle prototype 0.4.0

Restart the editor and reopen Goblin Scraper to load the updated plugin.

Build your machine as usual. Press B to deploy into the red camp. W/S drive, A/D operate steering, Shift drifts, F fires weapons and G operates mechanisms. Steering still needs a steering mechanism; this update does not bypass your construction.

Clear 18 red goblins in two minutes. Goblins pursue nearby machines and attack when close, reducing the prototype hull counter. Hull reaching zero or the timer expiring ends the attempt. Press B to return to the workshop, change your machine, and deploy again. Your assembled blocks are retained. Each deployment resets the wave.

Try a wide ram first, then a spinning blade or drill, then a cannon. Moving machine parts can hit enemies; cannon projectiles also register hits. Corpses use six physical bodies joined at the neck, shoulders and hips. Cutting hits or impacts above 7 m/s detach the nearest head or limb. At most ten corpses remain, and each expires after twelve seconds.

This is a combat prototype, not a finished battle system. Living enemies use simple pursuit and swept hit checks, rather than physical character controllers. Hull damage is an aggregate counter, not individual block damage. Dismemberment detaches existing body sections; it does not create arbitrary cuts or wound meshes. Other weapon effects, enemy navigation around cover, and faction progression remain future work.

The basic arena has boundaries, cover, deployment and camp markings, and red banners. It is generated by the builder at runtime. Main.bytescene enables BattlefieldEnabled and sets BattlefieldEnemyCount to 18; supported count is 1–24. Manual event actions include restartBattle and battleEnemyCount; conditions include battleRunning, battleWon, battleLost and battleKills.

Validated in the native engine: animated Idle/Walk/Attack, pursuit, powered-wheel ramming, jointed ragdolls, cannon hits, detached limbs, victory, defeat, retry and retaining the build. The 18-enemy scene update averaged 7.44 ms in the diagnostic run, excluding rendering; this does not guarantee 60 FPS.
